# Audit: where the warpSPH δ⁺ scheme consumes boundary information (2026-10-06)

Purpose: freeze the interface for **one** warpSPH δ⁺ scheme whose bodies are represented either by boundary particles (the existing mDBC path) or by an analytic tier of the package (working name `warpSPHBoundaries`: surface loop, box, implicit, SDF, volume).  Read-only audit of warpSPH @ 45375e8 (`schemes/deltaSPH.py`, `systems/weaklyCompressible.py`, `modules/{mdbc,shifting,surfaceDetection,pressure,momentum,deltaSPH}`, `rigidBody/`, `initializers/weaklyCompressible.py`) against `DeltaSPH2D.rhs / shift / no_penetration` and `FusedWall`.

## 1. Findings that shape the design

1. **Boundary particles are ordinary neighbours of every pair module.** Continuity, pressure, viscosity, density diffusion, the surface detector and the shifting sums run over one adjacency that contains kind-1 particles; `kinds` only enters the kernels as the operation-mode mask (`ki`, `referenceKinds`, directional operations) and in the final zeroing of non-fluid updates (`enforceUpdates`, `_capAndClampShift`, implicit shifting rows).  So the particle flavour needs **no per-module boundary code**, and with no kind-1 particles every boundary stage is a no-op (`stateHasBoundaryParticles`, cached on `config`).  The analytic flavour can follow the same rule: no analytic bodies, no calls.
2. **Every boundary is a `RigidBody`** (`configurations/rigidBody.py`; built from the case regions by `buildRigidBody`, stored in `schemeConfig.rigidBodies`, i.e. in the *config*, not in the state): centre, orientation, velocities, mass / inertia, rest-pose particle and ghost arrays, and an `sdf` callable.  Static walls are bodies with zero velocity.  `finalize` integrates them by explicit Euler with `dudt = dwdt = 0` (`integrateRigidBody`) and re-projects the particle / ghost rows (`updateBodyParticlesWCSPH`, which also writes `u_body` into the velocity rows that `computeBoundaryVelocities` reads, and a centripetal-only `boundaryAccelerations`).  **This is the unification point:** a `RigidBody` gets a representation (particles | analytic provider); the pose and velocities move into the integrated state (phase 4 here: `bodyPositions / bodyVelocities / bodyAccelerations`), and the particle rows become a derived view of the pose.
3. **Wall terms of the analytic flavour are additive** to the fluid-only module outputs, except in two places where a *decision* is taken from a sum: the surface detector (Barecasco cover vector and cone count: raw `C`, `count`, `allcount` are needed before the threshold) and the shifting (raw sum + the matrix `Mf + Mw` for the λ_min normals).  Those two need the fluid modules to expose their raw partial sums (our `sim/fluidkernels.py` already does; it is the upstream candidate) or to accept additive wall inputs.
4. **The two flavours differ in boundary-condition physics, not only geometry** (ghost-node extrapolation of ρ, p, v vs hydrostatic extrapolation with a clamp, free-slip mirror, no-slip flux): a shared interface can carry the geometry and the body state, the scheme keeps both BC models.

## 3. The aggregate the analytic provider returns (what `FusedWall.out` already is)

Per body, at the fluid particles (full-length arrays, masked to the near-wall rows, no index lists): `lam` (∫W), `G` (∇ of it), `Cov` (∫(x'−x)⊗∇W), `A(a1)` (hydrostatic), `cover`, `lap`, `tens`; `coneArea(c, half-angle)` → [2, N]; and geometry queries `signedDistance(x, supportMax, want_body) -> (d, n, hit, bodyIndex)`, `velocityAt`, `accelerationAt` on the body state.  Dimension-generic by construction of the interface (2D implemented).

## 4. Consequences and open questions

* **Protocol home:** structural `Protocol` defined in warpSPH (the scheme is its only consumer); the package depends on `warpSPHCore` only (ParticleState, adjacency, precision), warpSPH never needs to import the package for the particle flavour.
* **What changes in warpSPH:** (a) `RigidBody` gains a `representation` field and the pose moves into the system state; (b) `_deltaSPH_rhs` asks the providers at stages 06 / 08 / 11 / 12 / 13 / 16 (no analytic bodies → no call); (c) `detectFreeSurface` and `solveShifting` expose / accept the partial sums of finding 3; (d) `finalize` shifts + no-penetration at the final body pose (as `DeltaSPHSystem.finalize`); (e) the fluid pair kernels of `sim/fluidkernels.py` become modules (item 1 of the earlier list).
* **Consistency test between flavours (DONE 2026-10-06, `scene/particles.py`, plan 'Phase 5 prep 3'):** boundary particles can also be asked for the same aggregate (`Σ V_b W`, `Σ V_b ∇W`, `Σ V_b (x_b − x)⊗∇W`, …); the analytic integrals are their continuum limit, so `lam, G, Cov` must converge with the particle spacing: a cross-representation test that exists nowhere today.
* **Differences to settle, not hide:** explicit-Euler vs the integrator's exact end pose for accelerated bodies; `boundaryAccelerations` (centripetal only) vs `accelerationAt` (full); `zeros` / `constant` BC policies pin the boundary velocity (a moving body would see the absolute no-penetration law): to be mapped as closures, §5.  The placement question of the no-penetration shift is settled: impulse only.
* **Scope:** the incompressible, artificial-compressible, compressible and DFSPH schemes also read `rigidBodies` / kinds; the first target is the weakly compressible δ⁺ scheme only.
* **Decided 2026-10-06 (user):** `extended` BC (open-boundary inlets / outlets in diffSPH only, no case uses it) is dropped; `filterRegion` removes fluid particles that lie inside analytic regions; improved packing around analytic bodies is out of scope (the buoyancy test shows why it matters: lattice cut by a curved wall 0.6 - 0.78, conforming layers 1.00 - 1.01).
* **Audited in §5:** case construction (regions), the graph runner, `BoundaryToFluid`.  **Still open:** `extended` BC policy as a closure; `SdfRep` / `ImplicitRep` on the fused path; incompressible / ACSPH / compressible consumers of `rigidBodies`; how `filterRegion` (region overlap) treats analytic regions; mass / inertia of an analytic body (`buildRigidBody` sums particle masses: an analytic body needs them from the representation or the case) for two-way coupling.

## 5. Decisions (user, 2026-10-06) and the remaining audit items

**Decisions**
* **Exact integrator for bodies in warpSPH.** `integrateRigidBody` is explicit Euler with `dudt = dwdt = 0` at every call site, so a body's velocity can never change and an accelerated body gets the wrong pose; the library's `symplecticEuler` with the pose / velocity as integrated state (phase 4 here) is the general fix and closes that hole in warpSPH (stage poses, `boundaryAccelerations` from the full rigid-body acceleration, not the centripetal term only).
* **BC policies are closure choices the boundary integrals must support**, not particle-only features: `BCType` (`constant`, `noSlip`, `freeSlip`, `extended`, `zeros`) maps onto the aggregate flavour as the wall-velocity / viscosity / continuity closure (`freeSlip` = mirror, `noSlip` = flux at the contact point, `constant` / `zeros` = prescribed wall velocity, `extended` = to be derived).  The `zeros` / `constant` entries must then pin the wall velocity in the *relative* no-penetration law too (today they would see the absolute law for a moving body).
* **Only the impulse formulation of the no-penetration shift**: warpSPH has `'impulse'` (default since 2026-09-29), `'finalize'`, `'derivative'`, `'off'`; its own notes (`nopen-finalize-energy-pump`: `'finalize'` discards the step's pressure impulse on the corrected component while ρ keeps the compression, pumping lone wall riders; `'derivative'` can only oppose an into-wall velocity, never replace it) show the others are occasionally unstable.  `DeltaSPH2D` already has only `noPen = "impulse"` (applied in `finalize`, restitution `e = 1 − f`, never adds kinetic energy); the unified scheme keeps just that, which removes the `'derivative'` call at stage 16 and the `mdbcNoPenShiftMode` switch from the table of §2.
* **Frontend: the region declares the representation.** Today every boundary is `buildRegion(config, schemeConfig, sdf, RegionType.Boundary, kind=BCType)`: one `sdf`, sampled into kind-1 particles (+ ghost nodes), with `contour = find_contour(...)` computed alongside in 2D, and `buildRigidBody` turning each boundary region into one `RigidBody` (bodyId = region order).  The proposal: `ParticleRegion` gains `representation` ∈ `particles` (default, today) | `mesh` / `surface` | `sdf` | `implicit` (| `box` for the domain); `buildRegion` then samples particles only for `particles`, otherwise it creates the matching analytic representation and registers the body so that it hooks into the existing region machinery (`filterRegion` overlap handling, `buildRegionSystem`, per-region `kind`, `initialConditions`, the rigid body).

**Audit item: case construction** (`cases/weaklyCompressible.py`: `boundaryRegion(ctx, sdf, kind)`, `buildRegionSystem`, `paramShapeSdf` / `centredShapeSdf` shapes, `domainSDF`).  Mapping of the styles to the package, all from data a region already has:
* `surface` / `mesh` → `SurfaceRep` from `region.contour` (marching-squares polyline of the sdf, already computed in 2D by `find_contour`) or a user polyline; the tank domain is a clockwise loop (`background = 1`) or `BoxRep` (`domainSDF` is a box);
* `sdf` → `SdfRep` (sampled signed distance + the contour as `fallback` surface): both from the region;
* `implicit` → `ImplicitRep` from an analytic primitive (the named shapes of `centredShapeSdf`: circle, rectangle, …; a star / moon has no closed-form tier-3/4 model and falls to the polygon tier-2);
* `volume` stays a package-level option (nodal boundary data), no region counterpart yet.
Gap on our side (CLOSED 2026-10-06 for `ImplicitRep` disks and `SdfRep` as their exact polygon, plan 'Phase 5 prep 1'; `VolumeRep` and half planes still go through the oracle path): the **fused / graph-capturable path served `SurfaceRep` and `BoxRep` only**; `SdfRep` / `ImplicitRep` / `VolumeRep` go through the `sceneOperation` path (host syncs, no graph).  A frontend that offers `sdf` / `implicit` needs those two on the fused path (the tier-3 / 4 formulas are local per particle, so they fit the contraction kernel; the hard per-particle tier switch is a mask).

**Audit item: graph runner.** `GraphedIntegratorStep` captures the whole integrator step against the *state tensors* (every tensor attribute of `system.state` is a static input, outputs are cloned after replay, key = adjacency identity + state signature, bitwise validation against the eager step with automatic eager fallback, `deferHostTime`).  Consequences: (1) bodies must be **state** to be inside the graph, which the config-held `RigidBody` is not; with the phase-4 state (`bodyPositions / bodyVelocities / bodyAccelerations`) a moving analytic body is graph-clean by construction, and so would a moving particle body be; (2) a provider is graph-clean iff its calls are sync-free with fixed shapes, which `fixedadj` + `FusedWall` satisfy; per-position-set persistent buffers (our carry of the no-penetration wall evaluation: `FusedWall.copy_from`) are private to the provider and captured as static buffers, or are dropped in favour of the library's `priorStep` reuse; (3) the bitwise validation is the contract our `test_graphstep` already encodes.

**Audit item: `OperationDirection.BoundaryToFluid`** is *only* a neighbour-kind predicate inside the pair kernels (`checkDirectionality_j(referenceKinds[j], mode)`: boundary-to-fluid keeps neighbours of kind 1 and skips ghosts, kind 2) over the single particle adjacency; it does not build an adjacency, so there is nothing to reuse for an analytic provider's body-versus-queries adjacency (`fixedadj`).  What is shared is the vocabulary: `Scene.adjacency / precompute / sceneOperation` already take `OperationProperties(kernel, operation, gradientMode, operationMode=BoundaryToFluid)`.  The particle flavour's aggregate (the cross-representation test of §4) is exactly `warpOperation(..., BoundaryToFluid)` sums of `WarpOperation.{Density, Gradient, Covariance}`, which the package already uses for its oracle.
